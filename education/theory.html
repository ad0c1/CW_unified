§ 1

The object of study

Every psychometric or connectomic network reduces a stack of individual data to one number per edge. The carrier-weighted (CW) method asks what that reduction throws away — and keeps exactly the part that matters for community structure.

Let \(V=\{1,\dots,n\}\) be a finite set of items, alternatives, or ROIs, and let \(G=(V,E)\) be the complete comparison graph, so \(m=|E|=\binom{n}{2}\). For each edge \(e=(i,j)\), \(i<j\), and each observation unit \(k\) (a subject, a trial, a scan), we record a raw edge signal

\[ x^{(k)}_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}. \]

The physical meaning of \(x^{(k)}_{ij}\) is domain-specific:

DomainUnit \(k\)Raw edge signal \(x^{(k)}_{ij}\)
Typology items (GW)subject\(\pm 1\) key endorsement, paired via correlation
Preference / intransitivitysubject × domain\(+1\) if \(i\succ j\), \(-1\) if \(j\succ i\)
Brain functional connectivitysubjectFisher-\(z\) FC between ROI \(i\) and ROI \(j\)

Two structural properties must be tracked from the start. Symmetry: similarity-type signals satisfy \(x^{(k)}_{ij}=x^{(k)}_{ji}\) (GW, brain FC) and live on the undirected graph; preference signals satisfy \(x^{(k)}_{ij}=-x^{(k)}_{ji}\) and live on the oriented complete graph, which admits a combinatorial Hodge decomposition (§5). Directedness: whether an edge carries an orientation or only a magnitude.

Where CW sits

CW does not invent a new pipeline. It is a drop-in replacement for the edge weight consumed by the standard EGA → sparsifier (EBICglasso or TMFG) → Walktrap chain, evaluated by bootEGA.

Everything downstream — sparsification, community detection, bootstrap stability — stays exactly as the field already runs it.

§ 2

Why the pooled mean is not enough

The classical group-level edge weight is the pooled mean

\[ \bar{x}_{ij}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}x^{(k)}_{ij}. \]

This single number is blind to who produced it. An edge carried evenly by the whole sample and an edge carried intensely by a small minority — with everyone else at zero — can produce the same \(\bar{x}_{ij}\). Yet these are structurally different objects: the first is a population-shared relation; the second is a subgroup's signature, invisible once pooled.

The theoretical warrant comes from non-ergodicity. Molenaar (2004) and Fisher, Medaglia & Jeronimus (2018) showed that group-level statistical structure does not, in general, transfer to the within-individual level. CW is a structural (not temporal) application of that insight: instead of asking whether the group mean generalizes over time, it asks whether the group edge generalizes over people — and quantifies the answer edge by edge.

Existing heterogeneity-aware network methods (subgroup similarity, moderated networks, latent-class networks) all require pre-defined subgroup labels or estimate one network per latent class of respondents. CW requires neither. Its primitive is not the respondent but the (respondent, edge) pair: the same person may help carry one edge and be irrelevant to another. Cross-cutting carrier structure of this kind cannot be expressed as a subject-level mixture at all (§8).

One question

Correlation tells us how strong an edge is.

It does not tell us who made it strong.

CW is an estimator for the second question, computed without ever being told the groups.

§ 3

The carrier

The carrier \(c_{ij}\in[0,1]\) of an edge is its group-level support as a shared, non-accidental relation. Two canonical constructions cover the symmetric and antisymmetric cases.

3.1 Symmetric case (items, brain FC)

A trustworthy symmetric edge should be penalized for two failure modes: (a) contribution concentrated in a few subjects, and (b) contribution inconsistent with the edge's structural neighbours. One component for each.

Even-support component. Define the subject-level co-contribution

\[ S^{(k)}_{ij}=(x^{(k)}_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})(x^{(k)}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}), \qquad \text{Gini}_{ij}=\text{Gini}\!\bigl(\{\,|S^{(k)}_{ij}|\,\}_{k=1}^{N}\bigr). \]

Low \(\text{Gini}_{ij}\): many subjects contribute. High \(\text{Gini}_{ij}\): a few dominate. The even-support component is \(1-\text{Gini}_{ij}\).

Neighbour-alignment component. Let \(\mathbf{s}_e=\bigl(S^{(1)}_{ij},\dots,S^{(N)}_{ij}\bigr)/\|\cdot\|_2\) be the normalized subject-vector of edge \(e=(i,j)\). For every edge \(e'\) sharing a vertex with \(e\), compute \(\cos(\mathbf{s}_e,\mathbf{s}_{e'})\), and average:

\[ \bar{O}_{ij}=\operatorname{mean}_{e'\sim e}\cos(\mathbf{s}_e,\mathbf{s}_{e'}). \]

High \(\bar{O}_{ij}\) means the subjects who carry this edge also carry its neighbours — the footprint of a shared latent factor rather than an accident of sampling.

Definition · Symmetric carrier
\[ c^{\text{sym}}_{ij}=\bigl(1-\text{Gini}_{ij}\bigr)\cdot\max\!\bigl(\bar{O}_{ij},\,0\bigr) \]

Both components live in \([0,1]\); the product is the carrier. This is the exact form used in the GW CW-TMFG pipeline.

3.2 Antisymmetric case (preference flows)

Let \(P_{ij}=P\bigl(x^{(k)}_{ij}>0\bigr)\) be the fraction of units preferring \(i\succ j\). Then

Definition · Antisymmetric carrier
\[ c_{ij}=\bigl|\,2P_{ij}-1\,\bigr|\in[0,1], \qquad \sigma_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}\bigl(2P_{ij}-1\bigr)\in\{-1,0,+1\}. \]

\(c_{ij}\) is the majority strength on the directed edge — 1 if everyone agrees on the direction, 0 if the group splits 50/50 — and \(\sigma_{ij}\) records which direction the majority takes.

Reading the product

The two symmetric components answer different questions:

1 − Gini: is the support broad, or hoarded by a few?

max(Ō, 0): do the same people show up on adjacent edges?

An edge must pass both tests to keep its weight.

§ 4

The carrier-weighted edge weight

Definition · CW weight
\[ w^{*}_{ij}=c_{ij}\cdot g\bigl(\bar{x}_{ij}\bigr) \]

where \(g\) is a domain-appropriate magnitude transform. The carrier multiplies the pooled magnitude — it never replaces it.

Domain\(g(\bar{x}_{ij})\)Restriction
GW items\(|\rho_{ij}|\) (absolute tetrachoric correlation)Walktrap on positive \(w^{*}\) only
Intransitivity\(\sigma_{ij}\cdot|\bar{x}_{ij}|\) (majority-signed magnitude)antisymmetric flow, sign retained
Brain FC\(|\bar{r}_{ij}|\) (absolute mean Fisher-\(z\))Walktrap on positive \(w^{*}\); sign tracked separately

The full symmetric CW weight is therefore

\[ w^{*}_{ij} =c^{\text{sym}}_{ij}\cdot|\rho_{ij}| =\bigl(1-\text{Gini}_{ij}\bigr)\cdot\max\!\bigl(\bar{O}_{ij},0\bigr)\cdot|\rho_{ij}|. \]
Rule · Positive-part truncation is mandatory

Walktrap and modularity-based detectors interpret positive edge weight as intra-community attraction. Taking \(|w^{*}|\) on a signed weight silently converts an anti-correlation into a strong attraction and produces spurious cluster migrations — a bug class we have observed in practice. Whenever the downstream detector is Walktrap or modularity, use \(\max(w^{*},0)\), never \(|w^{*}|\).

The antisymmetric case keeps its sign, because Hodge decomposition (§5) is defined on oriented flows, not similarity weights.

§ 5

Decomposition: aligned and residual

Every CW-weighted edge signal admits a decomposition

\[ w^{*}=w^{*}_{\text{align}}+w^{*}_{\text{res}} \]

into a component consistent with a low-dimensional structural hypothesis and an orthogonal residual. The hypothesis is domain-specific:

DomainAligned componentResidual
GW itemsedges within a Walktrap communitycross-axis edges
Intransitivity\(w^{*}_{\text{grad}}=A\hat{s}\) — Hodge gradient of item scores\(w^{*}_{\text{cyc}}\in\ker(A^{\top})\) — cyclic flow
Brain FCintra-hemispheric edges (LL, RR)cross-hemispheric edges (LR)

5.1 CW-Hodge decomposition (antisymmetric case)

Let \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\) be the oriented incidence matrix of the complete graph, rows indexed by directed edges \(i\to j\) (\(i<j\)), with \(A_{(ij),k}=+1\) if \(k=i\), \(-1\) if \(k=j\), else 0. Given the CW flow \(w^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\):

\[ \hat{s}=(A^{\top}A)^{\dagger}A^{\top}w^{*},\qquad \mathbf{1}^{\top}\hat{s}=0, \] \[ w^{*}_{\text{grad}}=A\hat{s},\qquad w^{*}_{\text{cyc}}=w^{*}-w^{*}_{\text{grad}},\qquad A^{\top}w^{*}_{\text{cyc}}=0. \]

This is the ordinary combinatorial Hodge decomposition of Jiang, Lim, Yao & Ye (2011) — the novelty is not the decomposition but the flow it is applied to. The raw flow \(\bar{x}\) mixes group-mean magnitude with individual noise; the CW flow suppresses accidental agreement (low \(c_{ij}\)) and preserves structurally shared cycles (high \(c_{ij}\) with cyclic component).

Rule · One norm convention, asserted every run

All Hodge norms use the vector of the \(m=\binom{n}{2}\) uniquely oriented edges: \(\|w\|_E=\bigl(\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}^2\bigr)^{1/2}\). Never mix this with the Frobenius norm of the full antisymmetric matrix — since \(W_{ji}=-W_{ij}\), \(\|W\|_F=\sqrt{2}\,\|w\|_E\), and mixing the two inflates curl and cyclicity ratios by exactly \(\sqrt{2}\). (We caught precisely this error in a July 2026 teaching-page computation; it is now an automated assertion.) Every run must check

\[ \Bigl|\;\|w^{*}\|_E^{2}-\|w^{*}_{\text{grad}}\|_E^{2}-\|w^{*}_{\text{cyc}}\|_E^{2}\;\Bigr|<10^{-10}. \]
Summary statistics

Per unit \(k\): alignment \(G^{(k)}=\|w^{*(k)}_{\text{grad}}\|_2\); residual magnitude \(R^{(k)}=\|w^{*(k)}_{\text{res}}\|_2\); residual energy fraction \(\rho^{(k)}_{\text{res}}\); and carrier-weighted residual survival \(S^{(k)}=\|w^{*(k)}_{\text{res}}\|_E/\|\bar{x}^{(k)}_{\text{res}}\|_E\).

\(S^{(k)}\approx 1\) shows attenuation resistance — but by itself does not distinguish structure from sampling variation. That requires the permutation null of §7.

§ 6

The algorithm

The symmetric pipeline, end to end. Everything before step 07 is the CW contribution; everything from step 07 on is the standard EGA/bootEGA machinery, untouched.

CW-EGA (symmetric) in: X ∈ ℝ^{N×n}  ·  out: communities, P(k), τ
  1. standardize columns of X → Z // z_ki per item
  2. for each edge (i,j), i<j: // m = n(n−1)/2 edges
  3. S[k,ij] ← (z_ki − z̄_i)(z_kj − z̄_j) // co-contribution, all k
  4. gini[ij] ← Gini(|S[·,ij]|) // support concentration
  5. s_e ← S[·,ij] / ‖S[·,ij]‖₂ // normalized subject-vector
  6. for each edge e: Ō[e] ← mean{ cos(s_e, s_e′) : e′ shares a vertex with e } // 2(n−2) neighbours
  7. c[ij] ← (1 − gini[ij]) · max(Ō[ij], 0) // carrier, §3.1
  8. w*[ij] ← c[ij] · |ρ[ij]| // CW weight, §4 (ρ = tetrachoric/Pearson)
  9. w*[ij] ← max(w*[ij], 0) // positive-part rule (Walktrap downstream)
  10. net ← sparsify(w*) // TMFG or EBICglasso — unchanged
  11. communities ← walktrap(net) // unchanged
  12. bootstrap b = 1…B: // B = 200 resamples of units
  13. recompute steps 01–11 inside resample b // carrier included — no shortcuts
  14. record k(b), τ[ij] += 1[edge survives in b]
  15. report P(k = k*), τ/B, ARI vs. reference // bootEGA stability

The antisymmetric variant replaces steps 02–09 with \(c_{ij}=|2P_{ij}-1|\), \(w^{*}_{ij}=c_{ij}\,\sigma_{ij}\,|\bar{x}_{ij}|\), then applies the Hodge decomposition of §5.1 to \(w^{*}\) — and the bootstrap of steps 12–15 must recompute \(P^{(b)}\), the carrier, \(w^{*(b)}\) and its Hodge decomposition inside every resample, reporting percentile or BCa intervals for the cyclicity ratio.

Cost

The carrier adds \(O(N\,m)\) for co-contributions and \(O(N\,m\,(n-2))\) for neighbour alignment — for item banks (\(n\le 60\)) this is seconds, and the \(B=200\) bootstrap dominates wall-time exactly as it does in plain bootEGA.

§ 7

Validation and the pilot result

CW is evaluated with the field's own yardstick: the bootEGA stability distribution \(P(k=k^{*})\) over \(B=200\) resamples, plus adjusted Rand index against ground truth where truth is known. The baseline table is the community-detection bake-off of Christensen et al. (2024): GLASSO+UVA with Louvain, Fast-greedy, Walktrap, and parallel analysis. The claim CW must win is specific: the minority-carried scenario — where a real subcommunity's structure is carried by a fraction of the sample and pooled methods dissolve it.

3% → 19%
Pilot, GW-43 item bank (n = 605). Probability that bootEGA recovers the theoretically expected three-community solution, \(P(k=3)\): pooled correlation weight ≈ 3%; carrier-weighted ≈ 19%. Same sparsifier, same detector, same bootstrap — only the edge weight changed. This bootEGA-defined effect is the empirical hook of the preprint; Simulation 1 (companion page) tests whether it survives a world where truth is known.

Two inferential guardrails. First, all bootstrap inference recomputes the entire pipeline — carrier included — inside every resample; reusing the full-sample carrier across resamples silently understates uncertainty. Second, a permutation null must break coherent carrier structure while preserving every edge's marginal rate: a non-zero cyclicity or survival statistic is descriptive until it beats that null.

§ 8

What CW is not

Not subgroup-similarity analysis.

Methods in the Christensen (2023) line compare networks between pre-defined groups. CW extends the question from "do these labeled groups differ?" to edge-level, label-free carrier discovery: the groups, if any, are an output, never an input.

Not a mixture or latent-class network model.

Mixtures partition respondents into classes and fit one network per class. CW's primitive is the (respondent, edge) pair: the same respondent can carry edge A, oppose edge B, and be silent on edge C. Cross-cutting carrier structure of this kind has no representation as a subject-level mixture — this is a difference in kind, not in resolution.

Not a new community detector, and not sparsifier-specific.

CW changes only the weight; Walktrap, TMFG, EBICglasso and bootEGA run unmodified. A TMFG-sparsified sensitivity run is part of the evaluation precisely to show the effect is not an artifact of one sparsifier.

Not a claim that curl or residual is "real" by existence.

Carrier weighting cannot by itself establish that surviving structure exceeds sampling variation. Every headline statistic ships with a respondent-level bootstrap interval and a carrier-breaking permutation null (§7).
